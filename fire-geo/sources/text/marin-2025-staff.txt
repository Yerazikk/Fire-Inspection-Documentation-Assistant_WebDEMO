AGENDA DATE: November 4, 2025
TO: Marin County Board of Supervisors
FROM: Scott D. Alber, Fire Marshal
REVIEWED BY: Jason Weber, Fire Chief
SUBJECT: First Reading of the Proposed Ordinance to Amend Title 16
of the Marin County Code and adopt the 2025 California Fire Code and
the 2025 California Wildland-Urban Interface Code, with local
amendments.
RECOMMENDATION: Request the Clerk to read the proposed
ordinance by title only with amendments adopting the 2024
International Fire Code, 2024 Wildland-Urban Interface Code, 2025
California Fire Code, and the 2025 California Wildland-Urban Interface
Code, and to repeal all ordinances and parts of ordinances in conflict
therewith; and direct the Clerk of the Board to place the ordinance on
the Board’s November 18, 2025 Consent Calendar for approval.
SUMMARY:
The proposed Ordinance adopts the 2025 California Fire Code and the
2025 California Wildland-Urban Interface Code (California Code of
Regulations Title 24), including amends regarding fire prevention and fire
safety regulations in structures and on property located within the
jurisdiction of the Marin County Fire Department. These regulations are
currently provided by Ordinance 3775 (2022), and Ordinance 3453
(2006) of the Marin County Code (MCC) Title 16; and sets the effective
date of our local amendments to coincide with the January 1, 2026,
effective date of the California Fire Code and California Wildland-Urban
Interface Code regulated by statute. The proposed ordinance passage of
this proposed Ordinance will prevent a discrepancy between the
effective date of the State Codes and the effective date of our local
amendments, thereby preventing a window wherein our local
amendments are legally unenforceable.
This adoption process is required of the Marin County Fire Department
once every three years to stay current with the minimum requirements of

the State of California Building and Fire standards as determined by the
California Building Standards Commission. As such, over the last
several months, all Fire Prevention staff from the fire agencies within the
county met several times and cooperatively reviewed the proposed
amendments to make as consistent as possible our respective
amendments to each jurisdiction’s adoptive local ordinances.
The proposed modifications in this Ordinance are consistent with those
adopted in the 2022 Ordinance. However, the 2025 California Code of
Regulations, Title 24, introduces a significant change with the addition of
the Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) Code in Part 7.
The primary significant changes to Marin County’s amendments to the
Fire Code this code cycle are as follows:
• Chapter 16.17 of Title 16 (Marin County’s Wildland-Urban
Interface Code Ordinance) of the MCC is repealed and integrated
with Chapter 16.16 of the MCC (Marin County’s Fire Code
Ordinance).
• With the State’s adoption of 2024 International Wildland-Urban
Interface Code, Chapter 49 (Requirements for Wildland-Urban
Interface Fire Areas) of the California Fire Code was relocated to
Chapter 6 of the 2025 California Wildland-Urban Interface Code.
• The application of the provisions of the Wildland-Urban Interface
Code has been extended to include all Fire Hazard Severity
Zones (Local Responsibility Areas and State Responsibility
Areas), as well as designated Wildland-Urban Interface Areas.
POLICY FRAMEWORK: The County is currently enforcing the 2022
edition of the California Fire Code, portions of the 2021 International
Wildland-Urban Interface Code, both of which were adopted in
November 2022, as well as the 2006 International Wildland-Urban
Interface Code (adopted in 2007). The proposed Ordinance before the
Marin County Board of Supervisors repeals and replaces portions of
Marin County Code Title 16 (Marin County Fire Code and Wildland-
Urban Interface Code) with the 2025 State-mandated update to Title 24,
with certain exclusions and modifications.
Local amendments to Title 24 are allowed if the regulations are not less
restrictive than the State code, and if the governing body makes the
findings that the modifications are necessary based on local climactic,
geological, and/or topographical conditions.

EQUITY IMPACT SUMMARY: Adoption of the latest versions of the
California State Fire and Wildland-Urban Interface Codes are critical to
protecting the public from injury and death resulting from fire and other
associated hazards, allowing Fire and Building Code officials to apply
the latest codes and standards to the built environment. Analysis of
data regarding fires involving structures reveals that structure fire losses
disproportionately affect communities of low and moderate income, as
well as communities of color. Application and enforcement of the latest
Fire and Building Codes ensure a safer built environment protecting the
most vulnerable community members.
COMMUNICATIONS & ENGAGEMENT: Public notification has been
carried out in accordance with the Brown Act. Standard notice protocols
were followed through the Clerk of the Board and the County Executive
Office, including posting of the agenda, publication of the staff report,
and public access to supporting materials. These measures ensure that
the community and stakeholders have the opportunity to review the
changes to the ordinance and provide input at the scheduled Board
hearing. This Ordinance shall be published once adopted by the County
of Marin with the names of the supervisors voting for and against, in the
Marin Independent Journal.
CEQA ANALYSIS: Adoption of this Ordinance does not require review
under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) since it does not
constitute a “project” pursuant to CEQA Guidelines section 15378; and
under the “common sense” exemption CEQA Guideline section
15061(b)(3) because it can be seen with certainty that there is no
possibility that it will have a significant environmental effect.
FISCAL, FACILITY, & STAFFING ANALYSIS:
There is no impact to the general fund as a result of your actions. Fire
Prevention costs associated with this program are already allocated in
the Fire Department budget.
