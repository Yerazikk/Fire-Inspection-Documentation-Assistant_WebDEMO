## 2. Fire Behavior and Risk Assessment

### Primary Hazard: Vertical Fire Spread Through Ladder Fuels

The primary fire hazard is the lack of adequate separation between the lower hedge and the taller vegetation.

Ladder fuels are combustible materials that provide a pathway for fire to spread vertically from surface-level vegetation into taller shrubs or tree canopies.

In this photograph, the lower hedge creates a continuous band of combustible vegetation directly beneath the upper plants.

**Potential fire progression:**

1. **Ignition:** A wildfire ember, discarded smoking material, or another ignition source could ignite susceptible vegetation or accumulated dry debris.

2. **Fire spreads through the lower hedge:** Because the hedge is dense and relatively continuous, flames may spread along the vegetation, depending on fuel moisture, wind, and other conditions.

3. **Vertical flame extension:** Flames and heat from the burning hedge could reach the lower branches and foliage of the taller vegetation.

4. **Upper vegetation involvement:** Fire could spread into the taller vegetation, resulting in increased flame height, radiant heat, and potentially additional ember production.

5. **Exposure to surroundings:** Burning vegetation could create hazardous conditions near the neighboring structures and roadway identified by the inspector.

### Risk to Nearby Structures

The inspector identified a structure behind the vegetation.

If the vegetation ignites, fire spreading into the upper canopy could increase the heat exposure to the structure and potentially contribute to ignition.

The actual structure-to-vegetation separation cannot be measured using this photograph.

### Risk to Evacuation and Emergency Access

The vegetation directly borders a paved roadway.

If ignited, flames, radiant heat, and smoke could affect conditions along the road, potentially interfering with evacuation or emergency response.

The photograph alone does not establish that the road is an officially designated evacuation route or that its physical clearance is currently inadequate. The evacuation concern comes from the inspector's field assessment.

### Additional Vegetation Concerns

Some ground-level plants contain brown or apparently dried material. Scattered plant debris is also visible along the road.

These materials could contribute to available combustible fuel, although the photograph cannot establish their moisture content or combustibility.

### Overall Risk Assessment

**Inspector-confirmed condition:** Ladder fuel hazard requiring correction.

**Primary mechanism:** Vertical fire spread from lower vegetation into taller vegetation.

**Potential consequences:** Increased fire intensity, greater exposure to nearby structures, and unsafe conditions along the adjacent roadway.

**Severity:** Correction required based on the inspector's finding. A numerical risk rating or predicted flame height cannot be established from the photograph alone.
