Where we fit

First Due sells the system of record for fire prevention. Fire Aside sells the system of record for vegetation. Neither one reads the photograph. That is the whole opening — and it is narrower and better defended than it first looks.

The finding in one paragraph

The Fire Prevention brochure describes a compliance ledger with a form builder on top: checklist, code organizer, permit, invoice, occupancy record, map. Its unit of record is an occupancy — a business at a suite, with an occupant load and an IFC class. A hillside of brush has none of those fields, and a Berkeley fire chief has said so on the public record. The vegetation niche therefore is real, but it is not empty: Fire Aside has held it since 2021, is deployed county-wide in Marin — our own jurisdiction — and already does parcel records, resident reports and correction photos. What no vendor in either category publicly claims is the thing we built: a model that reads the inspector's photograph, derives the finding, picks the local citation, drafts the notice, and states plainly what the photograph cannot establish. We are not a better ledger. We are the judgment layer that sits on one.

What we are comparing

Three products, three different jobs

First Due
Fire Prevention
End-to-end prevention bureau suite: inspections, permitting, invoicing, occupancies, pre-planning. Eighteen modules across the wider fire platform. The buyer is a prevention bureau that issues permits and collects fees. Built for structures.
Fire Aside
DSI
Purpose-built defensible space and home-hardening inspection platform. iPad field app, parcel database, interactive resident report, ChipperDay and grant integration. 100+ agencies across 13 states. Built for vegetation — and it is the only one.
Fire Inspector V3
(ours)
One photograph plus one spoken field note in; a defensible finding out — fire-behaviour rationale, jurisdiction-gated citation, internal record, plain-language owner notice, the property shown corrected, and an explicit account of what the photograph does not prove. Built for the judgment, not the ledger.

The three are not substitutes. First Due and Fire Aside both answer who was inspected, when, did it pass, was it billed, where is it on the map. Ours answers what is wrong in this picture, why is it dangerous, which code makes it a violation, what exactly must the owner do, and what does done look like. Those are different questions, and only the second one has been getting harder.

What the Fire Prevention module actually contains

Read against the brochure page by page, the module is four things, and all four are custody of a record rather than analysis of a condition.

  • Inspections. A checklist builder. Every finding is one row: Pass / Fail / Citation / Corrected / N/A, plus a free-text comment and attachments. Their own copy: "Easily mark violations, make comments, and capture photos and videos."
  • Code Organizer. Import, create, activate, deactivate and link code sets — CFC, IFC, IBC, NFPA, state and local. Linking is manual and pre-curated: an administrator attaches sections to checklist items ahead of the season. Nobody at the property picks a citation from the condition in front of them.
  • Permitting and Invoicing. Permit lifecycle, certificates, fee schedules, GL codes, collections, billing reports. Substantial, and entirely outside our scope.
  • Occupancies and Pre-Planning. Business name, suite, occupant load, IFC class, sprinkler system, Knox box, ESRI mapping. This is the schema the whole module is built on.

None of the five reads an image. None touches an inspection finding, a code citation, a violation narrative or vegetation. Their flagship AI claim — describe the incident aloud and watch the fields fill — is the same shape as ours but pointed at the after-action report, not at the hazard. The word "vegetation" does not appear in the Fire Prevention brochure; neither does "defensible space," "wildland," "WUI," or "parcel." Nor does "AI."

The tell: "Virtual Inspections"

Their answer to inspector capacity is that the occupant shares a phone camera so an inspector can watch remotely. The inspector is still on the call. The camera is a telepresence device, not a sensor — which is precisely the ceiling a model that reads the frame removes.

Why a structure suite cannot be pointed at a hillside

This is structural, not a roadmap gap — and it is confirmed by a buyer, not by us

In April 2022 the Berkeley Fire Chief took a sole-source contract for Fire Aside to City Council. The justification is the clearest statement of our thesis available anywhere, and it comes from the customer side of the table:

"The Department has met with other major providers of fire prevention inspection software and discovered that all of them are built for the inspection of structures — which is completely different than the vegetation management inspections that the City is mandated to perform."

City of Berkeley, Consent Calendar Item 10, 26 April 2022

The same report notes the Department's current software "and the other leading fire inspection software available is not capable of meeting the business and operational needs," and sizes the mandate that broke it: a 83% increase — 7,184 additional inspections — to cover all Very High Fire Severity Zone properties in one mid-sized city.

The reason is schema, not features. An occupancy record wants a business name, a suite, an occupant load, an IFC class, a permit and an invoice. A parcel of brush has none of them, and the finding it produces is not checkbox-shaped:

The same two findings, as each system can hold them
FindingAs a checklist rowWhat is lost
Painted sprinkler head Automatic Fire Suppression → sprinkler obstructed → Fail → comment → photo Almost nothing. A trained inspector knows it in half a second and the remedy is singular: replace the head. The checkbox is nearly adequate — and so AI adds little here.
Ladder fuel Exterior → vegetation → Fail → comment → photo Everything. The violation is a spatial relationship between two plants; the remedy is a judgment about how much to cut. "Fail" cannot carry remove the lower tier, keep the oleander, cannot say how much counts as done, and cannot survive the owner's objection that you want to destroy their garden.

Those are the two examples already built. The sprinkler is the control case: it proves the output format works on a built-environment finding, and it also shows where the incumbent's checkbox loses little. Specialise where the checkbox collapses.

Fire Aside · the actual incumbent

Not First Due. This is the company we are really measured against.

Fire Aside is a Northern California company selling Defensible Space Inspections (DSI) — an iPad field app, a parcel-level database of vegetation and structure attributes, and an automatically generated resident report. It serves 100+ fire agencies, fire safe councils and HOAs across 13 states, and it is funded by the Marin Wildfire Prevention Authority for county-wide use after a 2021 pilot in Central and West Marin. That is the exact jurisdiction this repository's code corpus targets.

What it already does well — do not pitch against these

  • Parcel-level vegetation and home-hardening attribute capture, GPS-tagged, with photos
  • Automatic, prioritised resident report: photos of the exact issues, map images of where they are, multi-language, emailed or texted
  • Residents photograph the corrected work and upload it for approval; 30-day correction window
  • Integrations no one else has — ZoneHaven evacuation, ChipperDay community chipping, grant programs
  • Analytics on resident interaction and remediation behaviour
  • A claimed 40% reduction in time spent on re-inspections

What it does not publicly claim

  • Any use of AI, and any automatic analysis of a photograph. The photo is evidence shown to the resident; it is not read.
  • Fire code citation per finding — the report is a prioritised coaching checklist, not an enforceable record with a section number on it
  • Drafted hazard reasoning tied to the observed configuration
  • Any rendering of the property with the correction carried out
  • Any statement of what the evidence does not establish

Stated honestly: absence from marketing is not proof of absence from the product. Treat these five as unclaimed rather than confirmed missing, and verify against a live demo before putting any of it in front of a buyer.

Price anchor. Berkeley's contract: $20,000 one-time configuration plus $25,000 per year, two-year base with a four-year option, $160,000 ceiling over six years. Paid from Measure FF — a voter-approved parcel tax, not a prevention software line item. That tells us both the ACV to expect and where the money comes from.

Where we fit — the three defensible things

01

The after-image

The owner's own photograph with the lower hedge gone and the oleander kept — same camera, same light, nothing else tidied, no measurement invented off a single frame. The prompt discipline that makes it defensible rather than doctored already exists in the repo. For a homeowner being told to cut their plants, this converts "you want to destroy my garden" into "oh — just that row." No vendor in either category has anything like it, and it attacks the exact metric Fire Aside sells on: post-inspection compliance.

02

The uncertainty table

A list of what the photograph cannot establish — the distance to the structure, the species, the concealed dead growth, the controlling ordinance — each paired with the decision it hands back to the inspector. This is what lets a fire marshal sign the document. Anything that quietly asserts a distance from one frame dies on first appeal, and under Government Code §51186 agencies must now stand up documented inspection, enforcement and appeal procedures. An AI finding that volunteers its own limits is an asset in that proceeding; one that hides them is a liability.

03

The local-amendment corpus

Defensible space is the one area where the state code is a floor and every district amends it. The existing output already does the hard, correct thing: it refuses to name the enforceable citation without the jurisdiction, and distinguishes Marin County §604.6(7) from a Ross Valley town ordinance. First Due's answer is "import a code set and hand-link it"; Fire Aside's reports do not appear to cite sections at all. A curated per-district corpus with the amendments in it is slow, boring, and a genuine moat. Marin Ordinance 3775 is already ingested at 56 sections.

Those three compound. The citation makes the finding enforceable, the uncertainty table makes it survivable on appeal, and the after-image makes the owner comply without one. Each is weak alone and none is a feature a ledger company ships casually.

Why now

  • Zone 0 is law. The Board of Forestry adopted emergency ember-resistant-zone regulations under AB 3074 on 19 August 2026 — five feet from every exterior wall, deck, stair and pergola. All structures in State Responsibility Areas; occupied structures in Very High severity zones in Local Responsibility Areas. Pre-existing duties apply immediately; the rest phase in over three to five years.
  • The mandate multiplies inspections, not inspectors. One mid-sized city booked an 83% jump — 7,184 additional inspections — off a single earlier expansion. Zone 0 is larger and the work is finer-grained: mulch, firewood, fence attachment, what is under the deck.
  • Agencies must now own the appeal. Ordinance-level inspection, documentation, enforcement and appeal procedures, with notice before penalties. Defensibility stops being a nicety.
  • Zone 0 findings are photograph-shaped. Combustible mulch against siding, firewood under a deck, a wooden fence meeting a wall — these are visible in a frame and ambiguous in a checkbox, exactly like ladder fuel and unlike an exit sign.

Risks, honestly stated

  • The niche has a funded incumbent in our own back yard. Fire Aside is paid for county-wide in Marin by MWPA and sole-sourced in Berkeley. Selling the Marin story to a Marin agency means displacing software the regional authority already funds. Either sell outside Marin first, or sell through Fire Aside rather than against it.
  • We are a layer, and layers get absorbed. Photo-reading is a feature a record company can buy or build. The defence is the corpus and the appeal posture, not the model call — so the moat work is the boring work.
  • The dollars are modest. $25k a year per agency sets the ceiling. Reaching a real business means many districts, or a different buyer.
  • The money is not in a software line. Berkeley paid from Measure FF; Marin from MWPA. Grant and parcel-tax funding moves on its own calendar and dies with its own politics.
  • Agencies are the slowest buyer available. Insurers and utility vegetation programs are hungrier and already pay for parcel-level evidence. Worth testing before building a district go-to-market.
  • One wrong confident number ends the pilot. A single fabricated distance in a notice that reaches an appeal hearing is existential. The uncertainty discipline is not a differentiator we chose; it is the licence to operate.

What not to build

Permitting. Invoicing, fee schedules, GL codes, collections. Occupancy management. Pre-plans. ESRI mapping. Scheduling, assets, training, ePCR. A resident portal to rival Community Connect. ChipperDay and grant plumbing. Every one of those is a funded module at a competitor and a distraction here. Export into the system of record; do not become one.

Recommendation

What to do with this

Hold the position as the judgment layer for photograph-shaped vegetation findings, and make the next build about defensibility rather than coverage: the per-district amendment corpus, the appeal-ready record, and the after-image. Prove it on Zone 0 findings, where the mandate is new, the volume is largest, and no incumbent has a workflow yet. Then take it to whoever is most exposed to the appeal — which may well be an insurer before it is a fire district.
